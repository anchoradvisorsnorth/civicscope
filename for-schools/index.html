The SEA 1 squeeze

Indiana's Senate Enrolled Act 1 (2025) is tightening local property-tax revenue for school corporations and reshaping how operations-fund dollars — the same fund that covers capital projects — can be used in the years ahead.

Meanwhile the buildings aren't getting younger. Bond referendums fail. Low-bid procurement delivers change orders and risk. Deferred maintenance keeps growing. Boards want to act but don't have a clear path.

Why BOT fits schools

Build-Operate-Transfer is well-established in Indiana municipal projects and authorized for school corporations under IC § 5-23 — but still rarely used in K-12. Here's the shape of it:

Build
Developer designs, builds & finances
Operate
District pays from its operating budget
Transfer
Clean title & warranties to the district
  • No bond referendum. A private developer designs, builds, and finances the project; the district makes structured payments from its operating budget.
  • Price locked early. A Guaranteed Maximum Price is set before construction — no change-order surprises.
  • You pick your team. Choose your contractor and design professionals on qualifications and trust, not lowest bid.
  • Clean transfer. The completed facility transfers to the district with clean title and full warranties.
  • Public safeguards. Competitive RFQ, public-hearing board approval, payment and performance bonds, and district approval at every design milestone.
  • The board governs. The developer executes; the district retains approval authority at every stage.

Developers experienced in school BOT — like JBK Development — can walk a board through the process.
