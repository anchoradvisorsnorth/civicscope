The problem

Your code is public. Finding anything in it is not.

Most villages publish their ordinances, minutes and plans as folders of PDF files. To find the rule on commercial vehicles, a person has to know which folder holds it and then read a ninety-page document.

Many of those files are scans of paper. Nobody can search them at all: not the clerk, not a resident, not a search engine.

So the question comes to the front counter. The clerk becomes the search engine for the whole village, one phone call at a time.

What it does

It reads everything you publish, so nobody else has to.

01

Builds a library from what you already publish

We read the documents your municipality has made public and organize them into one searchable library. You do not rewrite, retype or upload anything.

  • Ordinances and the zoning book
  • Council and planning commission minutes
  • Plans, newsletters and notices
  • Your website: hours, contacts, events, meetings
02

Answers in plain English, with the source

A person types a question the way they would say it at the counter. Ask answers from your documents and links each one it relied on, so anyone can check the answer against the text.

  • Works on a phone, with nothing to install
  • Every answer shows where it came from
  • Figures read from a scanned page are marked
  • Suggested questions to get people started
03

Says so when the documents do not answer

Ask is built not to guess. When your published documents do not cover a question, it says so plainly and points the person to the village office.

  • No answers from outside your own documents
  • Your Code of Ordinances is weighted ahead of meeting minutes
  • Website answers show the date the page was read
  • Optional staff sign-in for the village's own page

The kind of questions it handles:

Can I keep chickens in town? Who is on the village council? When does the council meet? What are the office hours? Can I park a commercial vehicle at my house?
